# Sources and credits

The Birth Family Tool draws on public records. This page credits each source and says what was changed.
None of the record files contains Social Security numbers: they are removed when the files are prepared.

## Record files prepared for the tool

### Social Security applications and claims (NUMIDENT), 1936–2007

- **What it is:** the National Archives' public-use release of Social Security Administration records for deceased people: names, birth dates and places, parents' names, death dates.
- **What it really covers:** "1936–2007" is the Archives' title for the series, not its coverage. Measured in this copy, 88 in 100 dated deaths fall in the 1990s and 2000s, 9 in 100 in the 1980s, 2 in 100 in the 1960s and 1970s, and almost none before 1960. People who died before the 1980s are mostly absent, and so are their links to their children. About 72 in 100 records name a father.
- **Credit:** Wray, Anthony; United States Federal Security Agency, Social Security Board; and United States Social Security Administration. "Numerical Identification Files (NUMIDENT), 1936–2007." Ann Arbor, MI: Inter-university Consortium for Political and Social Research [distributor]. https://doi.org/10.3886/E207202V3
- **Also cite, as the depositor asks:** Hollingsworth, Alex, Krzysztof Karbownik, Melissa A. Thomasson, and Anthony Wray. 2024. "The Gift of a Lifetime: The Hospital, Modern Medicine, and Mortality." *American Economic Review* 114 (7): 2201–38. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20230008
- **Original records:** National Archives, Record Group 47, https://catalog.archives.gov/id/12004494
- **Licence:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. The National Archives states there are no access or use restrictions on the public-use files.
- **Changes made:** reduced to names, dates, places and parents' names. Social Security numbers were never copied; any nine-digit number found in a name field was blanked.

### Texas marriage and divorce indexes, 1966–2024

- **What it is:** both spouses' names and ages, date and county, one file per year.
- **Credit:** Texas Department of State Health Services, Vital Statistics Section. "Marriage/Divorce Indexes." https://www.dshs.texas.gov/vs/marr-div/indexes.aspx
- **Terms:** published by the State of Texas as free public downloads. The page gives no licence statement.
- **Changes made:** the state's yearly files, which use several layouts, were combined into one table per record type, with surname and first name split out for searching.

### Deceased veterans (BIRLS death file)

- **What it is:** name, birth and death dates, branch and service dates for about 20 million deceased veterans.
- **Credit:** U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, Beneficiary Identification Records Locator Subsystem. Obtained under the Freedom of Information Act and published by Reclaim The Records. https://www.birls.org and https://archive.org/details/BIRLS_database
- **Licence:** Public Domain Mark 1.0, as marked by Reclaim The Records.
- **Changes made:** reduced to name, dates, sex, first branch and service dates. Social Security numbers, file numbers and cause-of-death codes were not copied.

### Veterans cemetery burials

- **What it is:** burials of veterans and family members in national and state veterans cemeteries, each tied to the related veteran by name.
- **Credit:** U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, National Cemetery Administration. "National Cemetery Administration – Gravesite Locator." https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/national-cemetery-administration-gravesite-locator
- **Licence:** Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Public Domain Dedication.
- **Changes made:** reduced to the person's name and dates, cemetery and state, relationship to the veteran, and the veteran's name, branch and war.

## Searched live, only when the researcher asks

### 1950 census name index

- **Credit:** National Archives and Records Administration, *1950 Census*, https://1950census.archives.gov/ . The name index was produced by the National Archives with handwriting-recognition software and is corrected by volunteers. The records are public domain.
- **How it is used:** when the researcher presses "Look in the 1950 census", one person's name (and the state, if given) is sent to the National Archives' site and the census sheets naming that person are listed, with the names written on them. The tool identifies itself in each request and waits a second between requests, as the site asks. Nothing is stored.
- **Limits:** the index holds names by line only, not ages or relationships, and many names are misread. The researcher opens the page to confirm a household.

### Historic newspapers to 1963

- **Credit:** Library of Congress, *Chronicling America: Historic American Newspapers*. https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/
- **How it is used:** when the researcher presses "Search old newspapers", one person's name and years are sent to loc.gov and the matching pages are listed. Nothing is stored.

## Read only from what the researcher copies

The researcher opens these pages in their own browser and copies the text. The tool reads what is pasted, keeps the names and relationships for that one case, and does not keep the pasted text.

- **Ancestry** DNA match lists and match pages
- **Find a Grave** memorial pages
- **Obituaries** from newspapers, funeral homes, Legacy.com, GenealogyBank, HeritageHub and similar

The tool never visits these sites.
